Timing & Direction

Timing and direction describe the relationship between two hands or two props in a pattern. VTG gives that relationship a short label so a complicated movement can be named, compared, and understood at a glance.

What this page covers

A short guide to reading VTG labels, including the Quarter Timing and Quarter Placement ideas used in SpiroAnim. Use the app to explore the complete pattern library.

Creator spotlight

Big shout-out to Noel Yee. Noel created the VTG3 grid that organizes the timing-and-direction relationships in this guide. This reference builds on his work.

01

Timing

Timing asks: when do the two hands or props reach the same reference point? Think of their positions around a circle like hands on a clock.

T Together

They reach the reference point at the same time: 0 degrees apart.

S Split

One arrives halfway around the cycle from the other: 180 degrees apart.

Q Quarter

One arrives one-quarter or three-quarters of a cycle after the other: 90 or 270 degrees apart.

02

Direction

Direction asks: are the pair traveling the same way or opposite ways? It is separate from timing, so two things can share a position without sharing a timing relationship.

↻ ↻

S Same

Both travel in the same direction.

↻ ↺

O Opposite

They travel in opposite directions.

03

Build the label

The timing letter is always first. The direction letter is always second. Combine one choice from each section to read any two-letter relationship.

Timing+Direction= one label
TS
Together / Same: together in time, traveling the same direction.
TO
Together / Opposite: together in time, traveling opposite directions.
SS
Split / Same: split in time, traveling the same direction.
SO
Split / Opposite: split in time, traveling opposite directions.
QS
Quarter / Same: quarter time, traveling the same direction.
QO
Quarter / Opposite: quarter time, traveling opposite directions.
04

Read both circles

The hands travel around the larger arm circle. The props rotate around the smaller prop circle. Even when they complete the same number of rotations, those two circles can have different timing and direction relationships. VTG therefore gives each one its own label.

Hands first

The first label describes the relationship between the two hands.

/

Props second

The second label describes the relationship between the two props.

SO / SS reads: the hands are Split/Opposite, while the props are Split/Same. The slash always means Hands / Props.

With Quarter Timing, QO / QS reads: the hands are Quarter/Opposite, while the props are Quarter/Same. Each side is still measured independently.

05

Quarter Placement

Quarter Placement describes the shape made by the arms. It tells you where the arms are in relation to each other, not when they arrive there.

↻ ↻

Quarter-Same

The arms maintain an L shape as they travel around the pattern.

↻ ↺

Quarter-Opp

Other Opposite placements line both arms up on one axis. Quarter-Opp puts one arm on the vertical axis and the other on the horizontal axis, while the hands travel around their circles in opposing directions.

Keep the distinction: Quarter Timing describes a phase relationship; Quarter Placement describes an arm arrangement. They are separate qualities, and a pattern may have one without the other. Both use Quarter for the halfway point between Together/Same and Split.
