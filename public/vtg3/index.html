Where it came from

About the VTG 3

The VTG 3 was born out of the VTG 1 and the VTG 2. These were technical documents for spinning two props and were produced in PDF form. Both documents attempted to clarify understandings of compound circles, the types of patterns created within a given timing and direction, and how to transition between them. VTG 3 synthesizes all this knowledge into an elegant and coherent system.

The Vulcan Tech Gospel

The Vulcan Tech Gospel is a system designed to teach both the beginner and advanced spinner. The first goal of VTG 3 is to help the user understand the variety of flower patterns available in a given arm and prop timing and direction.

The second goal is to teach the user how to transition between patterns within a given timing and direction.

VTG 3 is constrained to

  • One pair of parallel planes.
  • Situations where props do not change speed individually.
  • Prop facings and hands start in two of the six snapshots, one from each axis.

Timing and Direction

Timing and direction refer to the relative timing - Tog and Split - and the relative direction - Same and Opp - of two props or hands in a pattern.

Timing is always listed first and direction second. There are four timing and direction relationships, with the abbreviation listed first below.

Arms first

The larger circle completed by the arm is listed first.

/

Props second

The smaller prop-circle timing and direction is listed second.

One of the largest challenges in VTG is recognizing that, because the props and arms complete the same number of rotations, they can have distinct timing and direction relationships. It may be challenging at first to recognize Tog timing when the arms are split apart from one another, or vice versa. For example, in SO/SS, the arms complete Split Time Opposite Direction while the props are in Split Time Same Direction.

Freeze the pattern

Snapshots

A snapshot is the image of a given pattern across the x or y axis. This includes both the arms on the larger circle and the props from the circle formed at the hand. There are six snapshots on each axis, making a total of 12 snapshots.

Pattern

Pattern refers to the image that the props of a given pattern create, stretched out over 360 degrees. The pattern indicates how many prop rotations per arm rotation you are attempting.

The patterns for the VTG are:

  • 1:1
  • 1:3
  • 1:5
